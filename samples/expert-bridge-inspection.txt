THE COURT: Mr. Langley, you may cross-examine.

BY MR. LANGLEY:

Q. You are Priya Dunlap, and you wrote the report on the Cinder Creek Bridge. Is that correct?

A. Yes. Priya Dunlap. Dunlap is D-U-N-L-A-P. I am a professional engineer, license number 19-4428. The report is dated October 9th, 2025.

Q. The bridge carries Osprey Avenue over Cinder Creek in Port Sable. You were there on September 18th, 2025?

A. I was. One day, from about 8:10 in the morning until 3:40 in the afternoon. It was dry. At noon the air was 68 degrees Fahrenheit.

Q. Who from Stonewharf Engineering was on the bridge with you?

A. Two technicians and me. They took photographs and held the tape. I made the measurements in Table 2.

MR. LANGLEY: I am marking the October 9th report as Exhibit 19. Forty-two pages, with the tables.

MS. IWU: No objection to the marking. We may object if a line is read out of context.

THE COURT: Exhibit 19 is marked for identification. You may inquire.

Q. Table 2 lists a crack in the deck soffit at panel C-4. What width did you write down?

A. Eighteen thousandths of an inch. That is 0.018 inches. I used a crack gauge, at the widest point I could reach from the inspection truck.

Q. Page 11 of your report says a crack under 0.020 inches is narrow. You still called the spot at C-4 serious?

A. I did, but not because of that width alone. The crack ran 7.5 feet, and the rebar cover there was three-quarters of an inch. The plans call for an inch and a half of cover.

MS. IWU: Objection, asked and answered. She has given the width and the reason.

THE COURT: Overruled. He asked why she chose that word. That is not the same question as the measurement.

Q. You drilled two cores on a bridge with four spans. Only two?

A. Yes. Each core was four inches across and eight inches long. Core 1 came from span 2. Core 2 came from span 3. Each of those spans is 86 feet clear. I did not core span 1 or span 4. Under span 4 the water was about 3 feet deep, and we could not set the drill.

Q. The 1978 plans show a design strength of 4,000 pounds per square inch. What did the cores break at?

A. Core 1 broke at 3,280 psi. Core 2 broke at 3,410 psi. The lab tested them on September 26th, 2025, seven days after they were drilled. The average of the two is 3,345 psi.

MS. IWU: Objection, asked and answered. The average is arithmetic on figures already in the record.

THE COURT: Sustained. Do not repeat the average. Next area, Mr. Langley.

Q. Page 18 gives a chloride number. Read the result and the unit.

A. At a depth of 2 inches, chloride was 0.22 percent by weight of cement. Our office treats 0.15 percent as the point where the steel may be at risk of corrosion. I did not make up that line. It is footnote 4.

MR. LANGLEY: Your Honor, may we have a short sidebar? Footnote 4 cites a manual I do not believe was produced.

THE COURT: Approach.

MR. LANGLEY: If she is using a written threshold, that manual should have been with the report. I do not want a standard appearing for the first time today.

MS. IWU: The footnote names the manual and the section. We produced it in the November set, document SW-14. He has had it.

THE COURT: Then it is not a surprise. The footnote may stand. No one will read the manual aloud unless it is offered. Step back.

Q. You gave the bridge an inventory rating factor of 0.86. A factor under 1.00 means it does not rate at the design load. Is that what your report says?

A. Yes. The design truck was an HS-20, a 36-ton vehicle in that older system. An inventory factor of 0.86 is why I would post the bridge or limit it. The operating factor was 1.12. That is a separate and less cautious number.

Q. You recommend a 12-ton posting. That figure is not in the September 18th field notes, is it?

A. It is not. I calculated it in the office on October 2nd, from the core strengths and the cover at C-4. The 12-ton posting is on page 30 of Exhibit 19.

MS. IWU: Objection, asked and answered. He has already asked whether the posting was written in the field.

THE COURT: Sustained. She has said it was an office calculation. Do you have anything new?

Q. One more subject. Stonewharf billed eight thousand four hundred dollars for the assignment, plus six hundred twenty dollars in lab fees?

A. That is invoice 773, dated October 15th, 2025. The engineering fee was $8,400. The lab fees were $620. The invoice does not change the measurements.

MR. LANGLEY: Nothing further.

MS. IWU: No redirect, Your Honor.

THE COURT: The witness may step down. We are in recess until 1:15.
