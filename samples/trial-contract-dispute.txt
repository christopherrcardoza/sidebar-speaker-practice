THE COURT: Please be seated. Mr. Prescott, call your next witness.

MR. PRESCOTT: Thank you, Your Honor. Plaintiff calls Calvin Hart.

THE COURT: Sir, remain standing for the oath. You may then take the stand.

BY MR. PRESCOTT:

Q. State your name, and spell your last name.

A. Calvin Hart. H-A-R-T.

Q. What business do you own?

A. Sable Creek Cabinets. We build and install custom cabinetry. The shop is at 90 Ledger Lane, in Brambleford.

Q. Did your company sign a contract with Northspan Builders in 2025?

A. Yes. I signed it on April 16th, 2025. The work was lobby millwork at the Kestrel Annex, a small office building on Pier Street.

Q. What was the price?

A. Forty-two thousand eight hundred dollars. That covered the base cabinets, the reception desk, and installation. Tax was separate.

Q. I am handing you a six-page document. What is it?

A. Our contract. My initials are on pages 1 through 5. My full signature is on page 6.

MR. PRESCOTT: Your Honor, plaintiff offers Exhibit 12, the April 16th contract.

MS. CHANG: No objection.

THE COURT: Exhibit 12 is received.

Q. After the work started, did Northspan ask for something more?

A. Yes. On May 29th their project manager, Rowan Quill, asked for extra maple shelving behind the desk. I spelled the name in my notes as Q-U-I-L-L.

Q. Did you write that down?

A. The same day. Change order 3 added sixty-four linear feet of shelving, at one hundred twenty dollars a foot. That comes to seven thousand six hundred eighty dollars.

MS. CHANG: Objection. The change order is not in evidence, and the answer assumes a signature we have not seen.

THE COURT: Sustained as to who signed it. He may describe what he saw. The jury will disregard any claim that a particular person signed until that is proved.

Q. What did you see the man do with the paper?

A. He read page 2. He asked me to move delivery up by one week. Then he signed the bottom line while I watched. I am not a handwriting expert. I only know what I saw.

Q. Was the extra amount paid?

A. The base contract was paid. The seven thousand six hundred eighty dollars was not. I sent invoice 2188 on July 8th, 2025, and a reminder on August 4th.

MR. OKAFOR: Your Honor, may we have a brief sidebar? I am concerned about a city memorandum that is not on the exhibit list.

THE COURT: Come up. Briefly.

MR. OKAFOR: If Mr. Prescott is about to read a number off that memo, we need a ruling first. It was never listed.

MR. PRESCOTT: I will not offer it. I can finish with the contract and the invoice.

THE COURT: Then stay with Exhibit 12 and the witness's invoice. No city memo. Step back.

MR. PRESCOTT: Nothing further, Your Honor.

THE COURT: Ms. Chang, you may cross-examine.

BY MS. CHANG:

Q. Mr. Hart, you are a cabinetmaker, not an engineer. Is that fair?

A. Yes. I measure, I build, and I install.

Q. Change order 3 says the shelves were required by the city. You never spoke to a city inspector yourself, did you?

A. I did not. Mr. Quill said the inspector wanted the shelves. I wrote down the reason he gave me. I have no city letter in front of me today.

Q. The unit price is one hundred twenty dollars a foot. Have you charged Northspan less for maple?

A. On a different job in 2024, yes. That job used a thinner board. This order was three-quarter-inch maple. One hundred twenty dollars is line 14 on our 2025 rate sheet.

Q. You still delivered on June 20th, though you say the extra was unpaid?

A. I did. The lobby opening was set for June 27th. I was not going to leave the base job unfinished over that balance. Section 9 of the contract says invoices are due in thirty days.

MS. CHANG: Objection, asked and answered, as to the contract terms.

THE COURT: Overruled. He added the opening date and the reason he delivered. Go on.

Q. The August 4th reminder went to accounts payable, not to Mr. Quill. Correct?

A. Correct. I used the address printed on their purchase order, number 5502.

MS. CHANG: Nothing further.

THE COURT: You may step down, Mr. Hart. We will take a fifteen-minute recess.
