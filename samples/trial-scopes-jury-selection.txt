THE COURT: Call them as they are drawn from the box.

MR. DARROW: Just the panel?

THE COURT: Examine them, Mr. Darrow, when they are drawn from the hat.

MR. DARROW: That’s all right.

THE COURT: All right. Let the sheriff draw them?

MR. DARROW: Yes, sir.

THE COURT: You may proceed, Mr. Sheriff.

SHERIFF HARRIS: W. F. Roberson, number twelve.

THE COURT: Come around, Mr. Roberson.

THE COURT: Are you a householder or freeholder in Rhea county?

PROSPECTIVE JUROR: Yes, sir.

THE COURT: You are a householder?

PROSPECTIVE JUROR: Yes, sir.

THE COURT: Have you formed or expressed an opinion as to the guilt or innocence of this defendant, John T. Scopes?

PROSPECTIVE JUROR: Well, to some extent, judge.

THE COURT: What do you base that opinion on, Mr. Roberson?

PROSPECTIVE JUROR: Rumor.

THE COURT: From some witness? Information from some witness, some of them who profess to know?

PROSPECTIVE JUROR: No, sir.

THE COURT: General rumor?

PROSPECTIVE JUROR: Yes, sir.

THE COURT: And do you think you can wholly disregard your opinion and go into the jury box, and try the case on the law and the evidence, and render a fair and impartial verdict?

PROSPECTIVE JUROR: Yes, sir.

THE COURT: I think he is a competent juror, gentlemen, I will pass him to the state first.

GENERAL STEWART: The state will take him.

MR. DARROW: We have the right to examine him?

THE COURT: Ask him anything you desire.

BY MR. DARROW:

Q. What is your business, what do you do?

A. A farmer.

Q. Do you own a farm of your own?

A. I am a renter.

Q. What is your age?

A. I am 30.

Q. Have you ever given special attention to this case?

A. Well, no more than just reading the newspapers.

Q. Are you satisfied that you could try it with perfect fairness to both sides?

A. Yes, sir, I think I could.

MR. DARROW: All right, we will take him.

THE COURT: All right. Have a seat, Mr. Roberson. Call the next man.

SHERIFF HARRIS: Number 34, J. W. Dagley.

THE COURT: Mr. Roberson, I intended to ask you, are you related by blood or marriage to Mr. Walter White, the prosecutor, or to John T. Scopes, the defendant?

PROSPECTIVE JUROR: No, sir.

THE COURT: Have you any interest at all in the case?

PROSPECTIVE JUROR: No, sir.

THE COURT: Mr. Dagley, are you a householder or freeholder of Rhea county?

PROSPECTIVE JUROR: Yes, sir.

THE COURT: Are you related by blood or marriage, to Walter White, the prosecutor, or to John T. Scopes, the defendant?

PROSPECTIVE JUROR: No, sir.

THE COURT: Have you formed or expressed an opinion as to the guilt or innocence of the defendant, John T. Scopes, on the charge of violating the antievolution statute?

PROSPECTIVE JUROR: Well, I can’t hardly say that I have.

THE COURT: Have you any fixed opinion, Mr. Dagley, any definite opinion as to his guilt or innocence?

PROSPECTIVE JUROR: No, sir.

THE COURT: Have you heard rumors about the case?

PROSPECTIVE JUROR: Rumor.

THE COURT: Have you talked to any person who is a witness in the case, or who professed to know the facts?

PROSPECTIVE JUROR: No, sir.

THE COURT: You would wholly disregard any impression you have regarding to the matter, Mr. Dagley, and go into the jury box—the case wholly upon the law and the evidence and render an impartial verdict to both sides?

PROSPECTIVE JUROR: Yes, sir.

THE COURT: He is a competent juror.

GENERAL STEWART: We pass him to the defendant.

BY MR. DARROW:

Q. You are a farmer?

A. Yes, sir.

Q. Near here?

A. What is that?

Q. Do you live near here, near the town?

A. Twelve or fourteen miles.

Q. Have you lived in Tennessee most of your life?

A. I was born and raised here.

Q. In this community?

A. Yes, sir.

Q. Have you ever known anything about evolution, or read about it?

A. I have not.

Q. You don’t know anything about it at this time?

A. No, sir.

Q. Are you a church member?

A. Yes, sir.

Q. Of what church?

A. Methodist.

Q. You have been for a good many years?

A. Yes, sir, a number of years.

Q. Have you ever heard it discussed in church?

A. No, sir.

Q. Did you ever hear your minister express himself on it?

A. No, sir.
